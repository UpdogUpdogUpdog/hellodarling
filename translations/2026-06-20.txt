Right now, I'm in Kesennuma, and I find myself feeling unexpectedly delighted.

I'm here in Kesennuma, of course, because of the "Kesennuma Okaeri Yose" event. Not that I'm trying to get overly excited or anything (laughs), but I arrived a day early, even though I'm just one of the audience members. It's strange yet perfectly natural that something which started in 2012 is still going strong here in 2026, and that fills me with real joy. Time has certainly passed; everyone seems to be doing well, though we've all gotten older in our own ways.

Even though it's the day before the event, I've met quite a lot of people. We drank coffee, took a little drive around town, and before we knew it, we were gathered together to chat over a meal in anticipation of tomorrow's main event. People from Kesennuma, members of Hobonichi, and Shinobu's staff all mingled and spent a long time together, and it was a lot of fun, including all the silly conversations. The delicious seafood and mountain dishes are delightful, but what truly brings me joy is the act of meeting and talking to people.

We physically transported ourselves here by bullet train and car, gathered in the same place, looked each other in the eye, talked with our mouths, listened with our ears, ate with our mouths, clapped our hands, laughed, took little bathroom breaks, and talked some more. This tangible (and wonderfully physical) gathering made me realize, once again, just how enriching it is. Every day, staring at some kind of screen and pondering weightless concepts like "AI this, AI that," the chance to "gather, see faces, and talk" felt like a deep breath of fresh air.

But no, today is still just the day before. Tomorrow is the real "yose." At the civic center, I'll meet yet another group of people. There are familiar faces who've come from afar, not just the locals. We're all gathering for that unique performance of Rakugo by Tatekawa Shinosuke. In the audience and lobby, we’ll spot familiar faces and call out to each other. Come to think of it, "yose" literally means "a gathering place." Meeting up and seeing each other healthy might just be one of the main purposes.

Thank you for visiting "Hobonichi" today. Kids are growing up, but so are young people, adults, and the elderly.
