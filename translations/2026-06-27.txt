How Much of a Watermelon Should You Eat?  
by Shigesato Itoi

I quite like watermelon and eat it often. Since becoming an adult, I’ve noticed that I no longer eat the less sweet parts near the rind. Of course, I don’t bother with the white part, but even with the red flesh, I leave behind the parts that aren't sweet. I wonder how my childhood self would react if he saw this. He’d probably say, “You can still eat that.” After all, back then, I did eat those parts. I still remember how I felt about it then. The red part was edible and was meant to be eaten. The most impressive way to eat watermelon was to consume it until the red completely disappeared. Though, I’m not sure who I was trying to impress.

But the part near the rind isn’t tasty. Sure, maybe some people like it, but it’s undeniably not sweet. As a child, that was one thing, but as an adult, I’ve come to think that deliberately eating the less tasty parts ruins even the sweet memories of the earlier bites. Loving watermelon and savoring its sweetness, only to end with the bland, flavorless rind—it just doesn’t sit right with me anymore. Over time, these thoughts led me to change how I eat watermelon. I've decided that watermelon isn’t just about eating the red and leaving the white; it’s more nuanced than that.

To be honest, the same goes for melons. With melons, the color transition is more subtle, so I stop eating when I judge that the sweetness is gone. I’ve never really checked how far others eat their watermelon or melon, nor have I ever been scolded for leaving some red part uneaten. Truthfully, I’ve given up on finishing the broth of ramen, even though it’s the most costly part. I'm sure there are people who might criticize me for this.

Thank you for visiting "Almost Daily" today. I’m not sure if I’m being stingy or extravagant—it’s hard to tell even for me.
