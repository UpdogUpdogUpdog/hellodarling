Saturday in Kanda, indulging in tonkatsu.  
Shigesato Itoi

・As the plate of tonkatsu arrives at my table, the first thought that crosses my mind is always the same: it's still piping hot. I start by gently separating three slices from the left side of the cutlet, the ones already scored with a knife, setting them slightly apart from the rest. A light squeeze of lemon, just enough to work its magic, followed by a cautious drizzle of soy sauce, careful not to overdo it. This is how I tackle the freshest, hottest piece. Ah, I almost forgot—a touch of mustard too.

The crispy coating gives way like thin ice under my front teeth. Then, the pork itself, the very heart of the dish, transforms into pure delight, spreading across my mouth. The aroma, the taste, the perfect tenderness—all of it dances over my tongue and cheeks, telling the story of tonkatsu. I chew with my molars, savoring each bite, my jaw and tongue guided by desire. Chew more, swallow, bite harder. As I bite down, I can feel the delicious fat seeping out, enhancing the flavor. I deliberately avoid looking too closely at the cross-section, but up to the second slice from the left, it's the fatty part—the pride of "special loin." As I relish the rich umami of the fat, I take a bite of rice, and the harmony of tonkatsu fat and white rice plays a symphony in my mouth.

Only then do I turn to the tonkatsu sauce. Again, careful not to overuse, I pour it over each slice, add a dab of mustard, and savor the crisp texture of the coating. By this point, I've settled into a rhythm, slowing my chewing pace, glancing upward and thinking, "Ah, it's delicious as always." When seated at the counter, a glance and a nod shared with the chef, the lady frying the cutlets, make for a silent acknowledgment. I extend my gratitude to the side dishes—cabbage, miso soup, pickled takuan and lightly pickled vegetables, and rice—that complement and elevate the tonkatsu experience. Once the plate is empty, it's time to pay the bill. In this familiar spot, I speak sincerely, bowing twice as I say, "It was truly delicious."

Thank you for visiting "Hobo Nikkan" again today. I'd already decided on Saturday's tonkatsu by Friday.
