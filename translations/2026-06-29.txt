Looking at the teddy bear got me thinking.  
— Shigesato Itoi

The saying "When you hate the priest, you hate his robe too" is quite well-known. It speaks to the human tendency to despise even the attire of a monk once you've taken a disliking to him. On the surface, the idea of "priest" and "robe" might seem unrelated to us personally, yet surprisingly, this is something everyone does.

You might dislike someone and, as a result, develop a distaste for their entire profession. Or hate an individual so much that you end up hating things they like. Or, you can't stand a sports team, so you loathe their mascot as well. We all fall into these feelings at times. (Conversely, sometimes you end up loving something just because someone you like loves it.) Thinking about this human tendency to let "When you hate the priest, you hate his robe too" take over, it seems important to recognize and control it. But, alas, the world doesn't work that easily.

Once you've labeled someone as the "priest (enemy)," everyone around them can start to look like "priests (enemies)" too, and you begin seeing everything as "robes (weapons of the enemy)." You might even start assuming everything the "priest (enemy)" says is a lie. Human history is full of this kind of behavior. In fact, it might have been even worse in the past than it is now. Not that the post-internet era is any better.

But, have you ever heard of someone who hates teddy bears or Winnie the Pooh just because they're afraid of or dislike real bears? I've yet to meet anyone who's mad at Iran and therefore hates Persian rugs. Most people, apart from a certain "very intense group," seem to be quite level-headed. For the record, I have a shellfish allergy, but I don't hate shrimp or crab. In fact, if I could be reincarnated, I'd love to eat lots of shrimp and crab.
