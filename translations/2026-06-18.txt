I want to open the windows wide, and keep the doors welcoming.
Shigesato Itoi

・The days are hot, yet the nights turn quite chilly.
　If you're not careful, you might get caught in the rain.
　That's the unpredictable nature of the rainy season.
　As long as the rain doesn't drag on endlessly, there's not much to complain about,
　and the lush green after a good soaking is truly beautiful.
　I find myself quite fond of it.
　Still, I'm getting my sunshade ready for the relentless heat of midsummer that's looming ahead.

　Lately, I've come to appreciate the simple act of occasionally opening a window,
　no matter the weather.
　In many new office buildings these days, the air conditioning is top-notch,
　but the windows don't open.
　I understand the reasoning—it's safer than risking a fall.
　But I struggle with spaces that lack windows, that disconnect from the outside world.
　Sure, you can manage temperature, humidity, and air quality,
　but on some subconscious level, I think people sense that "this place is sealed."
　In reality, it's a closed-off space.
　I wonder if it affects the people inside.
　Any living creature, if crowded into a tight, closed space, won't fare well.
　It's not just about air conditioning or climate control.
　The feeling of being open, of having a connection to the outside,
　I believe is critical to feeling comfortable in a space.
　You can eat and sleep in a prison cell, but the fact that it's a "closed space" you can't leave
　is what makes it a "punishment," isn't it?

　Freedom to come and go, an openness—
　if you're not constantly mindful of this, things tend to close up.
　It's easier to control things when they're closed.
　This is true even in the virtual spaces of the internet.
　Things shut down, conflicts arise, and it becomes stifling.

Thank you for visiting "Almost Daily" today.
My ideal is to have something like an "engawa"—a veranda—between the outside and inside.
