Who would have thought a draw could be so celebrated?

・My apologies for the chaos yesterday, but in the end, I managed to watch the World Cup on TV in good spirits.

When I think about it, the world sure has become a convenient place, hasn't it? We're talking about a soccer match taking place in Texas, USA. A team that traveled all the way from the Netherlands and a team from our distant home in Japan are competing, and there I am, an ordinary citizen, watching it all unfold in real-time. The progress of civilization in this regard is truly remarkable. No messenger or smoke signal could have handled this, and there's no way postal services or telephones could have provided live coverage. Friends tell me that even in the remote mountains of Nepal, the old ladies all have smartphones, and when it comes to communication, the world has reached a point of no return.

Perhaps, marveling at these things now might make people wonder why I'm stating the obvious, but indulge me—I'm an old-fashioned person who grew up drinking well water. If you like, I can tell you all about the outhouses we used. Just one coal brazier in a freezing classroom, and looking back, it feels like something out of a period drama. Knowing both those times and the environment of, say, 2026, I might just be luckier than most.

Even so, the echoes of that soccer game linger, and I find myself hungrily seeking more information on the subject. There's been a sudden surge in news about how "amazing Japan is." It seems various foreign commentators are explaining the strategies we had planned for the game. I may not fully grasp all the details, but as an amateur, I find it all incredibly fascinating. Among the highlights, Moriyasu-san giving tactical instructions on a "whiteboard" with handwritten numbers was something I absolutely adored—it was fantastic!

Thank you for visiting "Hobo Nichi" again today. I'm in trouble—my brain is struggling to get back to its usual routine.
