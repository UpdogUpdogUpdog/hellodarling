What should you be doing?

Shigesato Itoi

When a young person asks me, "What should I be doing with my life?" could I really give them an answer? I've been wandering through this question in my mind for some time now.

Thinking back, I realize that I never asked that question myself. To be honest, my main concern was just whether I could survive. The idea of "finding happiness" seemed like an overly ambitious and unfitting goal. Back then, I wasn’t too worried about things.

But enough about myself. I can understand why young people want to know, "What should I be doing?" It’s because they want to put in the effort once they have an answer. It’s like saying, "Give me a problem to solve, and I’ll work hard on it." In truth, finding that very problem is the real challenge, and the answer is simply discovering what problem you should be solving. But that’s a lifelong dilemma, and telling someone that doesn’t really help. I want to think about this not just for others, but for myself as well. What should young people be doing right now?

Looking back, the mentors in my life often said the same thing: "Read the classics." It might have been a kind of cliché. There was also advice like, "Experience a lot of heartbreak." In a way, these are safe pieces of advice. Yet, compared to more practical advice from a more recent era like "Get an MBA," "Build a network," or "Learn about computers," these suggestions don't seem any less valuable. Both "Read the classics" and "Experience heartbreak" are ways of saying, "Try to understand humanity." Well, I suppose I’ll keep pondering this question myself.

Thank you for visiting "Almost Daily" today. Figuring out "What should I be doing?" is something even adults and the elderly think about.
