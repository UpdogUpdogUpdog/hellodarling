I've tried to write this like a diary.

・In Kesennuma, I met almost everyone I know. Shinosuke Tachikawa's rakugo* acts like a magnet, drawing people in and gathering them together. That's the beauty of content—it should have that kind of pull. Even the Great Buddhas of Nara and Kamakura serve as magnets. Mount Fuji, too, is a colossal magnet.

Coming to Kesennuma around March 11th, I noticed that most of the people I met hadn't changed much. There was one guy, the most macho and nicest dude in Kesennuma, who had slightly altered his hairstyle. It had a gentle wave to it, and it looked pretty cool. When I pointed to his hair and said, "Hey, did you change something?", he shot back, as if prepared, "Just trying to look a bit more dashing." Over sixty and he's saying things like that. But, you know, it's these kinds of conversations that are fun.

That got me thinking. I've never really changed my hairstyle. Well, I have, but what I mean is, I haven't. I've been going to the same salon for about fifty years. The salon's the same, but the stylists change. To my mild embarrassment, it's always the manager at the time who takes over my hair. And since I've always just left it to them, my style occasionally changes, and that's why. The salon started in Harajuku and moved twice within Aoyama. Even with my eyes closed, my head would find its way there. About twenty years ago, the last manager who styled my hair went independent and opened their own place. I've followed him ever since—it's close by, which is convenient. I wonder if I should change my hairstyle to "look a bit more dashing" too. But probably not; I like leaving it to others. This isn't about being a "magnet," but rather about going with the "flow." There's a comfort in letting things move without force.

Even though I'm writing this like a game of word association, my mind's actually filled with thoughts about the World Cup today.

Thanks for visiting "Hobo Nikkan Itoi Shinbun" today. Who do we usually write diaries for, anyway?

---

*Rakugo is a form of Japanese verbal entertainment where a lone storyteller sits on stage and depicts a long and complicated comical story.
