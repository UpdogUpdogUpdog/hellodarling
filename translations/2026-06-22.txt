When you think about it, society is like a no-holds-barred, open-weight competition, isn't it?

In boxing, there are weight classes. They're divided quite precisely, and even a slight difference in weight can greatly affect the advantage or disadvantage a fighter has. It's not exactly the same, but in other sports, too, competitors are separated by categories like professional, college, high school, or middle school, based on age or experience. Additionally, men's and women's competitions are organized separately, taking into account differences in physique. This division is also seen in the realms of knowledge and academia. In math competitions, or games like shogi and go, participants compete within somewhat distinct fields.

However, once you step into society, it's as if everything is in an open-weight category. Differences in age, gender, experience, or basic training are no longer fundamentally considered. Figuratively speaking, whether it's an adult easily overpowering a child, or a grown man cornering a young person with logic, such scenarios don't pose an issue in the arena of work. Once you enter the arena, the competition, the rules dictate that there's no handicap for inherent differences in ability or the size of the organization. Whoever can win, wins. You can pour in as much money or manpower as you like as long as you succeed. Terrifying, isn't it? Shiver, shiver. Small, weak, not yet fully equipped—none of that matters. It’s all open-weight here.

Despite the ruthless nature of societal competition, the reason you, I, he, and she have managed to survive is, I believe, because we had the option of "not entering the match." Just as wise men avoid danger, the weak also steer clear of peril. We don't intentionally enter battles we're destined to lose, and we live by creating our own tiered competitions. It doesn't matter if someone says, "Nobita, are you running away?" We don't have to justify why we choose not to participate. Everyone knows it's fine to carefully pick and fight only those battles where you're ready to challenge or wish to win. The world may operate on a survival of the fittest rule in an open-weight sense, but even little monkeys and tiny medaka fish are thriving in their own way.

Thank you for visiting "Hobo Nichi" today. Just like in nature, there are niches in human society as well, aren’t there?
