"You don't need to know that."

・These days, a torrent of information rushes at us from all directions. Not just from the sea, the mountains, or the city streets, but mainly from the internet. "How about this? Don't you love this? This is the best!" It whispers in our ears, presenting itself as if it were the most crucial thing.

There's the world's major events, the industry scoop you should be aware of, weather updates, and tales of small creatures doing this or that. There are also the unsavory topics people hesitate to discuss openly, those who shout their emotions at you, the funny stories, and a never-ending supply of petty squabbles. Even if it's about strangers, when a fight breaks out on the street, a crowd gathers. You, too, might find yourself craning your neck out the window to catch a glimpse.

Incessantly, something or other is trying to get your attention. Thanks to some clever data management somewhere, information that each reader might find intriguing keeps popping up. As long as you're connected to the net, this won't stop. I know "Hobo Nikkan Itoi Shinbun" is just one part of that. I'm pondering "how to engage with the internet" just like everyone else, lost in the same maze.

Despite being very much in that maze, I've recently stumbled upon a little trick. As I read or follow a link, I pause and say to myself, "You don't need to know that." I've known it all along—most of it is stuff I don't need to know. Yet, once the fuse is lit, I can't help but chase after it. On a smartphone, those pesky, hard-to-close ads give you a perfect excuse to say, "You don't need to..." It's a way to disrupt the inertia or the habit. If you practice this consciously a few times, it becomes possible. Before saying, "Just throw away your smartphone," try giving yourself a pat on the shoulder. It's quite a good method.
