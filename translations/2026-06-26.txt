I want to learn as if I'm playing.  
Shigesato Itoi

・Learning something often feels quite similar to playing with something.  
  When you're learning tennis, chess, swimming, or dancing, if you're enjoying the time spent, it becomes a form of play.  
  However, if learning becomes a means to an end, it turns into a duty and ceases to be play.

  It all depends on the person doing it—what they think and feel deeply changes the experience.  
  Ultimately, it comes down to a difference in "perspective."  
  If you start off thinking, "I really don't want to do this, but I have to," it will inevitably feel dull.  
  Even in those cases, you might still improve and occasionally feel a sense of accomplishment, thinking, "I'm glad I did it, this feels great," and experience some joy.

  When there's a good teacher or mentor who makes learning enjoyable,  
  the learner's perspective shifts to one of enjoyment.  
  Then, even the tough parts or the occasional challenges can be embraced as just spices in the game.

  I never imagined that one could learn as if they were playing with someone who teaches well.  
  But apparently, in various learning environments today, such wonderful things are happening all over the place.

  I grew up in an era when learning was expected to be strict, sometimes involving scolding, with improvement lying beyond the struggle.  
  Because of that, I disliked learning new skills.  
  I'm envious of those who learn with joy.  
  It seems that theories and methods on the "teaching" side of "learning" have likely advanced, too.

  Even at my age, I want to experience learning that turns into play, regardless of how old I am.
